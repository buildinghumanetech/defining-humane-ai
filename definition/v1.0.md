# Humane AI, v1.0

*To be drafted Thursday, October 15th, 2026, in Malmö. This file is the working draft until it is tagged.*

## Definition

Humane AI is defined, designed, developed so as to strengthen human dignity across values.

## Dimensions

- **Individual**: fosters deliberate autonomy and capacity to flourish.
- **Society**: strengthens relational equality, social, and ecological resilience & cohesion. Facilitates connection between human beings.
- **Governance**: supports healthy, participatory relationships among both public & private institutions and between institutions and individuals.
- **Lifecycle**: rational and responsible use of resources, both material (environmental) and nonmaterial (intellectual, artistic, emotional).

## Definitions

*Plain-language readings of the terms above, drawn from the working session on Thursday, October 15th, 2026. The definition is the text. This section explains how the room used its words. It will change as the definition does.*

### The definition

**Human dignity.** The inherent worth of every person, in every culture. It belongs to individuals and to communities. We take the idea from the [Universal Declaration of Human Rights](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights). Its preamble recognizes "the inherent dignity and of the equal and inalienable rights of all members of the human family." Article 1 says "All human beings are born free and equal in dignity and rights." The session debated "strengthen" against "support". People already have dignity, so "strengthen" means AI should never cap it.

**Across values.** AI serves people with different values without ranking one above another. It shows more than one perspective, so the person keeps authorship of their own decision.

**Defined, designed, developed.** The duty covers how an AI system is made, not only how it behaves. The phrasing follows product-safety standards, which set a floor before they set a ceiling.

### Individual

**Deliberate autonomy.** A person's ability to reflect, choose, and author their own decisions. It includes the freedom to express themselves. Safety that shuts down self-expression defeats the purpose.

**Capacity to flourish.** The ability to thrive across the domains of a life. For the dimensions of flourishing, we point to the [Harvard Human Flourishing Program's framework](https://hfh.fas.harvard.edu/_files/ugd/889099_0b0c38b709df41e98ab0f01fe82bb913.pdf).

### Society

**Relational equality.** People and institutions relating to each other as equals. AI should not concentrate power or wealth in a few hands.

**Social resilience.** A society's ability to hold together under strain, built on mutual trust. The risk named was displacement: attachment to AI replacing attachment to people.

**Ecological resilience.** The health of the living systems we depend on. It includes the environmental cost of building and running AI.

**Cohesion and connection between human beings.** AI should move people toward each other, not away. The test is whether it supplements human relationships or supplants them.

### Governance

**Participatory relationships.** The people an AI system affects have a voice in how it is shaped, not only the individual using it.

**Healthy relationships among institutions and between institutions and individuals.** Public and private institutions relate to each other and to individuals as equals. AI should not entrench existing imbalances of power. Reversibility and oversight belong here.

### Lifecycle

**Lifecycle.** The whole arc of an AI system: design, development, deployment, and continuous evaluation. Behavior in use is one part of it. Environmental footprint and consent are others.

**Rational and responsible use of resources.** AI draws on material and nonmaterial resources. Use them in proportion to the benefit they bring, and without depleting what people and ecosystems depend on.

- *Rational* means proportionate. The benefit justifies the cost, and a lighter option was considered first.
- *Responsible* means accountable. The cost is measured, disclosed, and owned by whoever incurs it.
- *Material* resources are environmental: energy, water, minerals, and hardware.
- *Nonmaterial* resources are intellectual, artistic, and emotional. Intellectual means knowledge and other people's work, used with credit, consent, and fair return. Artistic means creative work and the livelihoods that depend on it. Emotional means people's attention, trust, and wellbeing, which are not to be mined for engagement.
