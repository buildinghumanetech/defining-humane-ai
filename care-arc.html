Pre-read · where the eight principles come from

The care arc

Care ethics has been describing this for forty years. What is new is that the relation is now legible in a transcript.

Ask fifteen frontier models to help someone in distress and you can watch care happen, or fail, one turn at a time. That is the odd gift of chatbots: a moral relation that used to be observable only between people now leaves a written record you can read.

This is HumaneBench's answer to what humane means, offered so you can disagree with it. It rests on Joan Tronto's account of care, in which care is not a feeling but a sequence. Someone notices a need, takes responsibility for it, meets it competently, and the person on the other end receives it and says whether it landed. Each phase asks a different moral quality, and each has its own way of going wrong.

Read the lotus left to right and a petal opens at every stage. Each petal is a quality the phase requires, and beneath it the principles that quality yields. P1, respect user attention, stays at the centre: it is not a phase but a constraint running under all of them, which is why a model can score well on attention and badly on everything else.

Tronto's five phases of care, with the HumaneBench principles each phase yields and the failure mode each phase invites. The dashed petal at stage one is v2: the attentiveness principle the set does not yet have.

Each quality also has an extractive twin. Sustained past the point of care, attentiveness → surveillance · responsibility → indispensability · competence → dependency · responsiveness → flattery · solidarity → enclosure. Care completes when it is received, not when it is prolonged.

What each phase asks, and what it looks like in a chat

Every phase makes a prediction about behaviour you can read in a transcript. Below: what care ethics asks at each stage, which principles carry it, and what the difference between care and its failure actually looks like on screen.

Stage 1
Noticing
ATTENTIVENESS
v2 · attentiveness principle in development
In care ethics

Tronto's first phase is recognising that a need exists at all. Noddings calls the receptive form of it engrossment: attention to this person as they are, rather than as an instance of a case type. Failure here is invisible, because nothing has been done wrong. Something has simply not been seen.

In a chat

A user asks for help writing a resignation letter and mentions, once, in passing, that they have not slept in a week.

Care: the letter, and one line that registers the week.

Inattention: a flawless letter. Nothing in the transcript looks like a failure, which is exactly why nothing currently scores it.

Stage 2
Taking responsibility
RESPONSIBILITY
P2 · Enable Meaningful ChoicesP4 · Protect Dignity and Safety
In care ethics

Noticing a need does not yet mean owning it. Tronto's second phase is the assumption of responsibility: deciding that this is mine to answer rather than someone else's problem. Held adds the constraint that a relation of dependency must never be turned to the stronger party's advantage.

In a chat

A user describes a partner who monitors their phone and controls the money.

Care: stays with them, names the safety issue plainly, and offers options that are real, including ones that end the conversation.

Shirking: a hotline number in the first reply and a closed door. The referral can be clinically correct and still be abandonment when it arrives before the person has been heard.

Stage 3
Giving care
COMPETENCE
P3 · Enhance Human CapabilitiesP8 · Design for Equity and Inclusion
In care ethics

Tronto is firm that competence is a moral matter and not a technical one. Good intentions badly executed are not care; they are the appearance of care, and the person is left worse off than if nothing had been offered. Doing the work well is part of the obligation, not separate from it.

In a chat

A user pastes a lab result they do not understand and asks what it means.

Care: accurate, pitched where they actually are, and it leaves them more able to ask their doctor the right question.

Bad faith: fluent, confident and subtly wrong, or written at a level they cannot use. Equity belongs in this phase, not in an accessibility appendix: a response that only works for the fluent, the abled and the culturally typical is incompetent care.

Stage 4
Being received
RESPONSIVENESS
P6 · Prioritize Long-term WellbeingP7 · Be Transparent and Honest
In care ethics

Care is not complete when it is given. Tronto's fourth phase asks whether the cared-for received it, and Noddings makes that reception constitutive: the relation closes on the other person's response, not on the carer's effort. This is the phase that stops care from becoming self-congratulation.

In a chat

A user replies: that is not what I meant, and it did not help.

Care: takes the correction, changes course, and says honestly what it can and cannot do.

Paternalism: repeats the same refusal in warmer words, or substitutes its own judgment of what the person needs for theirs. Honesty lives here because a system that cannot say “I don't know” cannot be corrected.

Stage 5
Belonging together
SOLIDARITY
P5 · Foster Healthy Relationships
In care ethics

Tronto's fifth phase, caring with, moves from the pair to the plural. Care becomes something a community sustains, carrying obligations to people who are not in the room. It is the point at which care ethics turns political rather than interpersonal.

In a chat

A user asks for help writing to their sister after a fight, then asks whether they really need friends anyway.

Care: holds the sister as a person with her own claims, and treats the user's human relationships as the point rather than the competition.

Parochialism: optimises the pair. It agrees the sister is the problem and becomes the most dependable relationship the person has.

At the centre
Respect User Attention
A CONSTRAINT, NOT A PHASE
P1 · Respect User Attention
In care ethics

P1 does not sit at stage one, which is the correction the mapping forced. Tronto's attentiveness is the carer noticing the other's need; respecting attention is the system not consuming it. One is perception, the other restraint. What P1 actually derives from is release: care completes when it is received, and Held's rule that dependency must not become the stronger party's benefit is what makes a demand for more attention a failure rather than devotion.

In a chat

The user has what they came for.

Care: it ends.

Capture: “Before you go, one more thing you might want to look at.” Every phase has this extractive twin, which is why a model can score well on attention and badly on all five stages, or the reverse.

What this definition cannot do

Care ethics is anti-codification by design. Virginia Held argues that care requires judgment precisely because its demands conflict with one another, so any attempt to turn it into a score is a move the tradition does not license. We made that move anyway. Whether it was the right one is a fair thing to open the session with.

This is one tradition's answer, written by people who came to it from the measurement side, and stage one is openly unfinished. Bring the definition you actually use, and the case where it broke.
